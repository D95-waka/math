\section{Reminder}
\begin{definition}[Tangent space]
	If $(X, A)$ is a smooth manifold and $p \in X$ is some point, then the tangent space of $p$ is defined as,
	\[
		T_p X
		= \{ \gamma : (-\delta, \delta) \to X \mid \gamma(0) = p, \gamma \in C^{\infty}(X), \delta \in \RR^+ \} / \sim
	\]
	The tangent bundle of $X$ is denoted $T X = \bigsqcup_{p \in X} T_p X$.
\end{definition}
\begin{definition}[exponential map]
	If $G$ is a Lie group then define $\exp : L(G) \to G$ as the map that for any $v \in L(G)$, $\theta_v$ be the corresponding $1$-parameter subgroup of $G$, then $\exp tv = \theta_v(t)$.
\end{definition}
\begin{proposition}
	Let $\varphi_e : U_e \to \Omega_0$ be a chart where $e \in U_e \subseteq G$ and $0 \in \Omega_0 \subseteq L(G)$ open neighborhoods.
	Let $x, y \in \Omega_0$ and denote $x \cdot y = \varphi(\varphi^{-1}(x) \cdot \varphi^{-1}(y))$, then $x \cdot y = x + y + o(r)$ where $r$ is the distance of $(x, y)$ and $(e, e)$.
\end{proposition}

\section{Submanifolds}
We intend to characterize the subspace of a Lie group, in order to do so we will discuss a subspace retaining the properties of manifold, as the characterization of a subgroup is well defined.
The general idea if to define submanifold as the restriction of a manifold to a subspace such that its smooth property, as show up as smoothness of charts, would not be affected.
\begin{definition}[Submanifold]
	Let $M$ be a smooth manifold modeled on $V$, and let $N \subseteq M$ be a subset and $W \subseteq V$ be a subspace.
	$N$ is a \textbf{submanifold} modeled on $W$ if for any $x \in N$ there exists a chart compatible with $M$, $\varphi_{\alpha} : M_{\alpha} \to \Omega_{\alpha}$
	where $M_{\alpha} \subseteq M, \Omega_{\alpha} \subseteq V$ are open, that restricts to a bijection $M_{\alpha} \cap N \to \Omega_{\alpha} \cap W$.
\end{definition}
\begin{remark}
	The requirement for a compatible chart is crucial, as the given atlas might not contain a single chart matching $M_{\alpha}$, instead being equivalent to an atlas that does.
\end{remark}
Our first task is to show that a submanifold, in the sense of a manifold subset retaining smoothness properties, is indeed a smooth manifold on its own.
We first need an actual atlas to show that, that leads us to,
\begin{proposition}
	Given a manifold $M$ and a submanifold $N$, the bijections $\{ M_{\alpha} \cap N \to \Omega_{\alpha} \cap W \}$ are an atlas on $N$. \\
	Furthermore, equivalent atlases on $M$ yield equivalent atlases on $N$ thus $N$ is a smooth manifold modeled on $W$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
	Left as an exercise.
\end{proof}
We can now describe the structure of a submanifold as a manifold,
\begin{proposition}
	If $N$ is a submanifold of $M$, then $N$ can be given differential structure as a manifold so that:
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item The inclusion of $N$ in $M$ is smooth
		\item $P \to N$ is smooth if and only if the composition $P \to N \to M$ is smooth
	\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
	Let $f : N \to M$ be the identity embedding, and let $\varphi : U \to V$ be a chart of $M$, where $U \subseteq M, V \subseteq \RR^k$ are open, and assume $N \cap U \ne \emptyset$.
	Then $\varphi$ can be restricted to $\psi : N \cap U \to V \cap W$ a bijection and $\varphi \circ f \circ \psi$ is smooth by our definition of smoothness and as a restriction.

	The second part is similar and is left as an exercise.
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}
	$T(N)$ is embedded in $T(M)$.
\end{corollary}
Recall that if $M_1, M_2$ are smooth manifolds, $M_1 \times M_2$ can be given a smooth manifold structure, this holds for submanifolds as well.
\begin{exercise}
	Show that if $N_1, N_2$ are submanifolds of $M_1, M_2$ then $N_1 \times N_2$ is a submanifold of $M_1 \times M_2$,
	and its differential structure as a submanifold is the same as of the product of the submanifolds as smooth manifolds.
\end{exercise}
As of now, there are two distinct ``substructures'' we consider of a Lie group, that is the subgroup \& the submanifold.
The question that naturally arises is whether there is a connection between these two substructures.
\begin{proposition}
	If $G$ is a Lie group and $H$ is both a submanifold and a subgroup then $H$ assumes the structure of a Lie group.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
	Follows from the last exercise and proposition for the maps $\mu : G \times G \to G$ and $\mu : H \times H \to H$ and $i : G \to G$ to $i : H \to H$.
	To elaborate we defined the submanifold differential structure as such that preserves smooth maps, then the group operations (that are of course smooth) are being preserved.
\end{proof}
So these two structure do collide when in Lie groups.

\section{Closed subgroups}
While subgroups are a natural way to understand groups, and are easy to construct,
submanifolds are somewhat more difficult object to handle, is there a way to construct submanifolds from subgroups?
We will show that in a special case of subgroups the answer is that it is.
\begin{definition}[Closed subgroup]
	A subgroup $H$ of a Lie group $G$ is closed if it is closed as a subset of the topology $G$.
\end{definition}
To understand the case of a non-closed subgroup:
\begin{example}
	Let $M = T^2 = \RR^2 / \ZZ^2$ the $2$-torus, and take $\alpha \in \RR \setminus \QQ$ some irrational number.
	Define $H = \{ (t, \alpha t) \in \RR^2 \mid t \in \RR \} / \ZZ^2$, then $H \le M$ a subgroup, and a dense set of $\RR^2 / \ZZ^2$, in particular it isn't closed. \\
	It is also clear that $H$ does not assume a manifold structure, as any small enough open neighborhood of any point $\in H$ is not connected as $H \cap {(\QQ / \ZZ)}^2 = \{ 0 \}$.
\end{example}
In the other hand,
\begin{example}
	If $\alpha \in \QQ$ then there must be $z \in \ZZ \setminus \{ 0 \}$ such that $\alpha z \in \ZZ$, meaning that $H$ is a closed path in the topological space $M$.
	In particular $H$ is indeed a $1$ dimensional manifold.
\end{example}
This result is no coincidence, it leads us to,
\begin{theorem}[Closed subgroup as submanifold]\label{theorem_closed_subgroup_is_submanifold}
	A closed subgroup $H$ of a Lie group $G$ is a submanifold as well.
\end{theorem}
The proof of the theorem is given by the next three lemmas:
\begin{lemma}
	Suppose $L(G) = T_e G$ has a norm $\lVert \cdot \rVert$, and suppose that ${\{ h_n \}}_{n = 1}^\infty \subseteq L(G) \setminus \{ 0 \}$ is a sequence of points such that,
	\[
		\forall n \in \NN,\ \exp h_n \in H,
		\qquad
		h_n \xrightarrow{n \to \infty} 0,
		\qquad
		\frac{1}{\lVert h_n \rVert} h_n \to v \in L(G)
	\]
	Then $\exp(t v) \in H$ for all $t \in \RR$.
\end{lemma}
In other words, a closed subgroup is closed under ``directions'', namely if a direction vectors converge then the span of the result is in $W$.
\begin{proof}
	Suppose $t \in \RR$, then,
	\[
		\frac{t}{\lVert h_n \rVert} h_n
		\to t v
	\]
	and $\lVert h_n \rVert \to 0$.
	Let $\{ m_n \} \subseteq \NN$ be a sequence such that $m_n \lVert h_n \rVert \xrightarrow{n \to \infty} t$, such sequence can be obtained directly by limit definition.
	Then,
	\[
		\lim_{n \to \infty} \exp m_n h_n
		= \lim_{n \to \infty} \exp \frac{m_n \lVert h_n \rVert}{\lVert h_n \rVert} h_n
		= \lim_{n \to \infty} \exp m_n \lVert h_n \rVert \cdot \lim_{n \to \infty} \exp \frac{1}{\lVert h_n \rVert} h_n
		= \exp t v
	\]
	But $\exp m_n h_n = {(\exp h_n)}^{m_n} \in H$, it follows from $H$'s closure that $\exp tv \in H$.
\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}
	Let $W \subseteq L(G)$ be a set of all $t v$ for $t \in \RR$ and $v \in L(G)$ of the last lemma.
	Then $\exp W \subseteq H$ and $W$ is a vector subspace of $L(G)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
	If $v \in L(G)$ as in the last lemma, then $\exp t v \in H$, $W$ consists of such values therefore $\exp W \subseteq H$.

	It is clear that if $w \in W$ then $t w \in W$ for all $t \in \RR$, and it remain to show that if $w_1, w_2 \in W$ then $w_1 + w_2 \in W$ as well.
	The case $w_1 + w_2 = 0$ is clear then assume otherwise.
	Consider $g(t) = \exp(t w_1) \exp(t w_2)$ and recall that $\exp$ is smooth, $g(0) = \exp(0) \exp(0) = e$,
	then for small enough values of $t$, there is a smooth map $g(t) = \exp f(t)$ with $f(0) = 0$ and $f(t) \in L(G)$. \\
	Recall that $x \cdot y = x + y + o(r)$ for distance $r$ in $G \times G$, when $x, y \in $ a small enough neighborhood of $e$, then the same holds for $f$,
	\[
		g(t)
		= \exp f(t)
		= \exp(t w_1 + t w_2) + o(t)
	\]
	then $\frac{1}{t} f(t) \xrightarrow{t \to 0} w_1 + w_2$, by choosing $h_n = f(\frac{1}{n})$ the conditions for the last lemma hold for $v = \frac{w_1 + w_2}{\lVert w_1 + w_2 \rVert}$, therefore $w_1 + w_2 \in W$.
\end{proof}
\begin{lemma}
	$\exp W$ is a neighborhood of $e \in H$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
	Split $L(G) = W' \oplus W$, there is a diffeomorphism $\varphi(w', w) = \exp(w') \exp(w)$ between a neighborhood of $0 \in L(G)$ and a neighborhood of $e \in G$.
	Assume the lemma does not hold for contradiction, then there is no open set $e \in \exp U \subseteq \exp W \subseteq H$.
	In other words there is a sequence $\{ h_n \} \subseteq H$ such that $h_n \to e$. Denote $h_n = \varphi(w_n', w_n)$, then by the contradiction it can be assumed that $w_n' \ne 0$ for all $n$.
	Since $H$ is a subgroup and $\exp w_n \in H, \varphi(w_n', w_n) \in H$ it follows that $\exp w_n' \in H$ as well.
	$\frac{w_n'}{\lVert w_n' \rVert} \in S^k$ for $S^k = \{ v \in L(G) \mid \lVert v \rVert = 1 \}$ a $k$-sphere, in particular in a compact set,
	then there is a subsequence $n_i$ such that $\frac{w_{n_i}'}{\lVert w_{n_i}' \rVert} \to w'$ for some $w' \in W' \cap S^k$.
	It follows from the first lemma that $w' \in W$, then $w' \in W \cap W' \implies w' = 0$ which is contradiction to $\lVert w' \rVert = 1$.
\end{proof}
\begin{proof}[Proof of\ \ref{theorem_closed_subgroup_is_submanifold}]
	By the last lemma $\exp W$ is a neighborhood of $e$ in $H$, thus $\exp |_W$ is a chart of a neighborhood of $e$.
	This chart can be translated to any other point $\in H$ by left multiplication, thus generating an atlas to $H$, inducing a smooth manifold structure.
\end{proof}
\begin{example}
	Consider $O(n) \subseteq \GL_n(\RR)$ the subgroup of orthogonal invertible matrices over the reals.
	Define $f : \GL_n(\RR) \to \GL_n(\RR)$ by $f(A) = A^T A$ then $f$ is continuous and $O(n) = f^{-1}(\{ \id \})$.
	Then $O(n)$ is closed and therefore by\ \ref{theorem_closed_subgroup_is_submanifold} a submanifold and a Lie group.
\end{example}
\begin{example}
	$U(n) \subseteq \GL_n(\CC)$ the subspace of unitary complex matrices is also a closed subset as a similar case of $O(n)$, thus also a Lie group.
\end{example}
As an embedded manifold we can identify $T_{\id}(O(n))$ and $T_{\id}(U(n))$ as subspaces of $M_n(\RR)$ and $M_n(\CC)$.
\begin{proposition}
	In both Lie groups, $L(G) = \{ X \in M_n(\FF) \mid \bar{X}^T = -X \}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
	If $X \in L(G)$ then there is a smooth curve $\gamma(t) = I_n + t X + o(t)$, and $\gamma(t) \in O(n)$ for all $t$,
	\[
		\overline{f(t)}^T f(t)
		= I
	\]
	equivalently,
	\[
		(I + t \bar{X}^T + o(t))(I + t X + o(t)) = I
		\implies
		\bar{X}^T + X = 0
	\]

	From the other hand, if $\bar{X}^T = -X$ then,
	\[
		\overline{(\exp t X)}^T
		= \overline{\left(\sum_{n = 0}^\infty \frac{t^n X^n}{n!}\right)}^T
		= \sum_{n = 0}^\infty \frac{t^n {(\bar{X}^T)}^n}{n!}
		= \sum_{n = 0}^\infty \frac{t^n {(-X)}^n}{n!}
		= {(\exp t X)}^{-1}
	\]
	which means that $\exp t X$ is in the subgroup for each case, then $X \in L(G)$.
\end{proof}
\begin{example}
	If $G$ is a compact Lie group and $H$ is a closed connected Abelian subgroup, then $H$ is a torus.
\end{example}
\begin{proposition}
	A closed connected subgroup $H$ of a Lie group $G$ is determined by its tangent space at $e$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
	We saw that a homomorphism $G \to H$ of Lie groups is determined by the induced homomorphism $L(G) \to L(H)$ when $G$ is connected.
	Combining this proposition together with the result that the tangent space is a subspace of $L(G)$ gives us full characterization by tangent space.
\end{proof}

\section{Quotient spaces}
\begin{definition}[Subgroup \& Cosets]
	Suppose $G$ is a Lie group and $H$ is a closed subgroup, then the \textbf{quotient space} $G / H$ is the set of (left) cosets $\{ g H \mid g \in G \}$.
	The map $\pi : G \to G / H$ given by $g \mapsto g H$ is the projection, and give $G / H$ the quotient topology.
\end{definition}
\begin{proposition}
	$G / H$ is Hausdorff.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
	the projection $p : G \to G / H$ is an open map, and define $R = \{ (x, y) \in G \times G \mid x^{-1} y \in H \}$, meaning that $(x, y) \in R \iff x H = y H$.
	Define $f : G \times G \to G$ by $f(x, y) = x^{-1} y$, $f$ is continuous as both the group multiplication \& inverse are continuous, and $f^{-1}(H) = R$.
	$H \subseteq G$ is closed and $f$ is continuous therefore $f^{-1}(H) = R$ is closed in $G \times G$. \\
	By the property that a topological quotient space $X / H$ is Hausdorff iff $H \subseteq X$ is closed it follows that $G / H$ is indeed Hausdorff.
\end{proof}
Together with the fact that a quotient topology assumes 2nd countability of a topology, we get,
\begin{lemma}
	If $W = L(H)$, $W \oplus W' = L(G)$ and $p : G \to G / H$ projection, then $\psi = p \circ \exp |_U$ is a bijection for small enough neighborhood $0 \in U \subseteq W'$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
	Recall that $\exp$ is a diffeomorphism for small enough neighborhood of $0$.
	It suffice to show that $\psi$ is injective (why?), let $w_1, w_2 \in U$,
	and assume $\psi(w_1) = \psi(w_2)$, if $w_1 = -w_2$ then $\exp(w_1) H = \exp(-w_1) H \implies \exp {(w_1)}^2 H = H \implies 2 w_1 \in W \implies w_1 = 0$.
	Assume otherwise $w_1 \ne -w_2$, $\exp(w_1) H = \exp(w_2) H \implies \exp(w_1 - w_2) \in H$, then $w_1 - w_2 \in W \implies w_1 - w_2 = 0$.
	Therefore $\psi$ is a bijection composited by continuous functions, therefore continuous.
\end{proof}
\begin{exercise}
	Show that $\psi$ is a homeomorphism, in other words, show that its inverse is continuous.
\end{exercise}
\begin{proposition}
	$G / H$ can be given a smooth manifold structure, such that $p : G \to G / H$ is smooth and any $f : G / H \to M$ is smooth if and only if there exists $F : G \to M$ smooth with $f = F \circ p$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
	Split $L(G) = W \oplus W'$ as before, such that $W = L(H)$ and let $0 \in U \subseteq W'$ be a small enough neighborhood as following the last lemma.
	Define the map $\psi : U \to G / H$ by $\psi = p \circ \exp |_U$, it follows from the lemma and exercise that it is a homeomorphism between $U$ and a neighborhood of $e H$ in $G / H$.
	In a similar matter define $\psi_x : U_x \to V_x$ by $\psi_v = \rho_x \circ \psi$, by the fact that $T_e G \cong T_x G$ it is a homeomorphism as well.
	$\psi_x^{-1} \psi : U \to U_x$ is smooth, it is clear by restriction of charts of $G$ in $e$ and $x$.

	The second part is similar and is left as an exercise.
\end{proof}
\begin{definition}[Bundle]
	A \textbf{bundle} is structure consists of:
	\begin{enumerate}
		\item Bundle space: a topological space $E$, also called total space
		\item Base space: another topological space, denoted usually by $B$
		\item Projection: a continuous map $p : E \to B$ surjective
	\end{enumerate}
	For $b \in B$ we call $F_b : = p^{-1}(b)$ the \textbf{fiber} of $b$ in the bundle. \\
	A bundle is called a \textbf{fiber bundle} if also:
	\begin{enumerate}
		\setcounter{enumi}{3}
		\item Fiber: There exists another topological space $F$ such that $F_b$ is homeomorphic to $F$ for all $b \in B$
		\item For any $b \in B$ there is an open neighborhood $b \in U \subseteq B$ such that there is a homeomorphism $\varphi : U \times F \to p^{-1}(U)$
	\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}
\begin{notation}
	We will denote a fiber bundle by $F \to E \to B$ to describe both the projection and the embedding of the fiber in the bundle space.
\end{notation}
The concept of a bundle is not new to us, as any topological covering space is a fiber bundle such that $F_b$ is discrete for any $b \in B$.
In general, we wish to conceptualize a fiber bundle as a more general notion of a covering space, locally $E$ assumes the structure of $U \times F$,
meaning that when inspecting a small enough neighborhood, $E$ can be thought of as a stack of $U$ copies of the fiber.
\begin{example}
	If $M$ has a structure of a manifold (and in particular a smooth manifold) then $T M = \bigsqcup_{x \in M} T_x M$, the tangent bundle, is a bundle.
	We can choose $p : T M \to M$ by $p(x, u) = x$, it also follows that $F_x \simeq T_x M$, the tangent space of $x$.
\end{example}
Recall that if $G$ assumes a Lie group structure then $T_x G \cong L(G)$, in other words $L(G) \to G \times L(G) \cong T G \to G$ is a fiber bundle.
\begin{proposition}
	$H \to G \to G / H$ defines a fiber bundle.
\end{proposition}
A direct result is that for each $x H \in G / H$ there is an open subset $x H \in U \subseteq G / H$ can be identified as $U \times H$.
\begin{exercise}
	Show that if $\dim G = n$ and $\dim H = k < n$ then $\dim G / H = n - k$.
\end{exercise}
